Short biography

I am a Senior Software Engineer with over nine years of experience blending backend engineering and data science. I currently work for a leading car rental company in the automotive industry based in Munich, where I architect, lead, and deliver scalable microservice solutions.

My core expertise sits at the intersection of two disciplines:

Backend engineering. I specialise in robust, high-performance backend systems using Java and Spring Boot. I have led end-to-end service design, deployment, and modernisation across distributed systems.

Data-driven engineering. I leverage a deep background in data science and machine learning (Python, predictive models) to approach engineering with an analytical mindset, ensuring system design is optimal for data processing and business intelligence.

My current tech stack includes AWS, Kubernetes, Docker, Kafka, Postgres, and gRPC/REST.

I am constantly setting myself out to learn new things, whether from online articles and discussions, to books and podcasts. Many people do not understand why I do so, especially at late hours of the day, but it is very simple to me: learning new things is something that I find great joy in doing.

Long form biography

When I was about fourteen years old, I used to spend countless hours playing a game called “Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas”. This game had a huge community behind it, many of whom dedicated their free time to create modifications which either changed or extended the game beyond its capabilities. One day I found a modification which implemented online multiplayer features, something which the game lacked and was wished for greatly. I instantly got hooked, as from being used to playing on my own, I was suddenly opened up to thousands of people who I could play with, just thanks to a single modification. But this modification did a lot more than just provide online multiplayer features. It also allowed people to use a scripting language called Lua to change the behaviour of the game. Whether it was simply spawning something out of nowhere, or even creating whole new game modes which people could play together, this scripting language could allow anyone to do it all. This amazed me, and got me interested in learning the language to be able to do all the cool things these other people were doing. A few months later, this online multiplayer modification went open source, an area completely new to me at the time, and got me interested in open and collaborative programming. This is when I realised that programming was what I wanted to do after college.

In college, I discovered the field of data analytics. I liked the idea of being able to derive insight from raw data. I knew that I would want to get into this field but my bachelor degree did not have any units on data analytics. This is why I set it upon myself to learn and master the field on my own and base my thesis on it. I knew that if I were to do this, I would have something to show for my work. Having found the area of finance to also be interesting to me, I thought it would be a great idea to merge the two fields together. This is when I discovered the field of computational finance, a branch of applied computer science that deals with problems of practical interest in finance.

While researching for a thesis topic, I found a particular field called algorithmic trading, a method of executing a large order using automated pre-programmed trading instructions accounting for variables such as time, price, and volume to send small slices of the order out to the market over time. Being challenged with the fact that I knew very little about finance, I set it upon myself to read heavily on many terms and techniques available to me online, including podcasts which I would listen to on my drive to college. This helped me greatly in getting up to speed on ideas which I could apply towards my thesis. I set out to learn Python, which is a common high-level programming language used in this area, and built a fully automated strategy leveraging machine learning models to decide whether to trade certain US equities. Although this strategy did not beat the market, having made a profit of over 100%, I was still pleased knowing that my first attempt could yield a profit at all, let alone one of 83%.

After college, I joined a company as their first HR Data Analyst, a field that was completely new to me but a challenge I did not shy away from. Plagued with HR data not being unified across the departments in the HR group, I was tasked with handling not only the data from my department, but also that belonging to the whole group. I set up data entry standards, tidying checks, reporting, and visualisations, all of which I fully automated. I was able to apply what I learnt for my thesis, and build tools which helped the business take back control of their data and understand it more through actionable insights. It was also my duty to handle the GDPR compliance for all HR departments across the group, a task which involved a lot of collaboration with various departments across various locations.

That was the beginning of a nine-year journey. I transitioned from data analysis into backend engineering, and eventually into senior-level system architecture. Today I work for a leading car rental company in the automotive industry, based in Munich, where I architect, lead, and deliver scalable microservice solutions. My work sits at the intersection of two disciplines: backend engineering, where I specialise in robust, high-performance backend systems using Java and Spring Boot, and data-driven engineering, where I leverage a deep background in data science and machine learning (Python, predictive models) to approach engineering with an analytical mindset.

I believe that the best engineers are the ones who never stop learning. Whether it is through online articles and discussions, books and podcasts, or late-night experiments, learning new things is something that I find great joy in doing.
